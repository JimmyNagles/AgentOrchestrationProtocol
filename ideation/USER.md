# TARGET USER
> Who exactly are we building for? The more specific, the better the product.

---

## Primary User

**Who are they?** Solo founders and indie hackers who are building SaaS products using AI coding agents. They might have a day job or be full-time on their project. They're technically capable but not necessarily senior engineers — many came from vibe coding.

**What's their day like?** They wake up thinking about their product. They open 2-4 terminal windows, launch Claude Code or Cursor, try to remember what they were working on yesterday. They spend 10-15 minutes re-establishing context per agent. They assign tasks, alt-tab between terminals, review output, deal with merge conflicts, lose track of what each agent is doing. By evening they've shipped some code but feel like they spent more time managing than building.

**What do they care about most?** Speed and momentum. They want to feel like they're shipping fast. They care about building something real, not tinkering with tools. They want the feeling of running a company, even if it's just them and their AI agents.

**What's their skill level?** Range from "learned to code with AI last month" to "senior dev who realized agents let them move 5x faster." The common thread is they're willing to direct AI rather than write every line themselves.

---

## A Day in Their Life (Before Your Product)

1. Morning: They open their laptop, try to remember where they left off yesterday.
2. They open Claude Code, realize the session is gone. Copy-paste the cold-start message. Wait for it to re-read the codebase.
3. They assign a backend task. Open another terminal for a frontend task. Open Gemini for a design review.
4. 30 minutes in, they can't remember what each agent is doing. They alt-tab between 4 terminals.
5. One agent overwrites a file another agent was working on. Merge conflict.
6. They hit a rate limit on Claude. Now one agent is dead for 30 minutes. The dependent task can't start.
7. End of day: some code shipped, but they feel scattered. No record of what was decided or why. Tomorrow they'll start from scratch.

---

## What Would Make Them Switch?

They'd switch if they could see all their agents in one place, launch them with one click, track what each one is doing visually, and feel a sense of progress and momentum — like they're running a real team. If it felt like a game instead of a chore, they'd never go back.

---

## Who Is This NOT For?

- **Enterprise teams** with existing DevOps and CI/CD pipelines — they have their own orchestration
- **Developers who only use one agent** — no coordination problem to solve
- **People who don't use AI coding tools at all** — the market hasn't arrived for them yet
- **Framework builders** (CrewAI, LangChain users) — they're building agent systems, not using agents to build products
